Quantifying Selection Bias in NFL Field Goal Attempts: A Comparison of Inverse Probability Weighting and Pseudo-Label Augmentation
________________________________________
Abstract
•	Problem: Field goal models are trained on a biased sample of attempts selected by rational coaches, ignoring the latent difficulty of declined kicks ("non attempts").
•	Methods: We evaluate three modeling frameworks on 2015–2024 NFL data: a Baseline GLMM (M1), an Inverse Probability Weighted model (M2), and a Pseudo-Label Augmented model (M3).
•	Key Findings:
1.	Rationality: Propensity scoring reveals a structural discontinuity—coaches effectively filter out low-probability kicks, creating a "Rationality Gap."
2.	Prediction Victory: The IPW model (M2) achieves the best in-sample fit (Brier 0.0986 vs Baseline 0.1006), proving that weighting difficult kicks improves descriptive accuracy.
3.	Evaluation Insight: The Augmented model (M3) reveals a "Pessimism Gap" of ~3.4% in the 50+ yard range, where it predicts lower success rates than the biased observed sample.
•	Conclusion: While IPW (M2) is superior for operational forecasting, Augmentation (M3) provides the most "honest" baseline for player evaluation by accounting for the kicks coaches refused to take.
________________________________________
1. Introduction
•	1.1 The Selection Bias Problem: Kickers are evaluated only on the kicks they are allowed to take. Elite kickers attempt difficult kicks; weaker kickers are protected.
•	1.2 The "Rational Agent" Hypothesis: Do coaches filter out random variance, or do they strictly adhere to a probability threshold? (Foreshadowing the "Rationality Gap" result).
•	1.3 Research Goal: To determine if correcting for this bias improves predictive sharpness (Brier Score) or merely alters calibration (Bias).
2. Data & Methodology
•	2.1 Data Universe: NFL Play-by-Play (2015–2024). 4th down plays in FG range.
•	2.2 The Propensity Model pi: Multinomial Logistic Regression (Kick vs. Punt vs. Go). Used to generate weights 1/pi
o	Pi: clipped (2/98%) and within 3 SD
•	2.3 Model Hierarchy:
o	M0 (Distance Only): The naive control.
o	M1 (Baseline): GLMM (Random Effects on Kicker:Season & Stadium) trained on Attempts only.
o	M2 (IPW): Weighted GLMM. Up-weights rare/difficult attempts to simulate the missing population.
o	M3 (Augmented): Trains on Attempts + PAT + non-attempts. Non-Attempts >33y with high xFG (>0.7) are treated as Misses with a fixed weight (0.10).
3. Results
•	3.1 The Rationality Check (Visual Evidence):
o	Finding: Minimal overlap between attempts and non-attempts below 30% win probability. Coaches are efficient filters.
•	3.2 The In-Sample Leaderboard (The "Fit" Victory):
o	M2 (IPW) is the clear winner on Brier Score (0.0986).
o	Interpretation: Weighting the training data forces the model to respect the variance of difficult kicks, improving discrimination between Makes and Misses.
•	3.3 The Calibration Surprise (The "Fairness" Victory):
o	M3 (Augmented) achieves the lowest Calibration Error (0.0111 vs Baseline 0.0163).
o	Interpretation: Explicitly labeling "declined" kicks as "misses" corrects the Optimism Bias inherent in the filtered sample.
4. Discussion: The Pessimism Gap
•	4.1 Quantifying the Gap (The "Money" Plot):
o	At 50+ yards, the Baseline predicts 67.6% success (matching the biased sample).
o	The Augmented Model predicts 64.6%.
o	The Gap: The 3.0%–3.4% difference represents the "hidden difficulty" of the kicks that were never taken.
•	4.2 Discrimination Analysis (Makes vs. Misses):
o	M2 (IPW) widens the probability gap between Makes and Misses by 2.3 percentage points compared to Baseline.
o	It achieves this asymmetrically: it is slightly less confident on Makes, but significantly more accurate on Misses (lower surprise).
•	4.3 Operational vs. Evaluative Models:
o	For Betting/Game Planning: Use M2 (IPW). It best describes the world as it currently exists (where coaches protect kickers).
o	For Player Ranking: Use M3 (Augmented). It evaluates kickers against the "True Universe" of difficulty, not the "Protected Universe."
5. Conclusion
•	Summary: Selection bias in the NFL is structural and significant. Standard models overestimate the success probability of random long-distance kicks by ~3.4%.
•	Final Recommendation: Analytics departments should utilize a two-track modeling approach: IPW for in-game win probability and Augmented Pseudo-Labeling for contract negotiations and roster evaluation.

Kicker Model: Proposed Shift to a π-Only Selection Framework (with IPW/AIPW)
Why Change from xWPA: A two-layer decision stack built on xWPA: estimate win probability for (a) kicking and (b) going for it, multiply by success probabilities, then compare. This is great for decision audits (“should they have kicked?”), but it bakes in a rational-coach assumption and adds modeling burden: four WP heads + a 4th-down conversion model before we even correct selection bias in the make% model.
What I’m proposing: Move selection correction and evaluation onto a single, descriptive axis:
	Directly model π(X) = P(FG attempt | game state X) on the full, kickable 4th-down universe.
	Use Inverse Probability Weighting (IPW) to fit the kick success model on attempted kicks only but calibrated to the entire decision space.
	Evaluate with Augmented IPW (AIPW) so our reported accuracy reflects both attempted and non-attempted states—without fabricating outcomes.
This keeps the modeling focused on what coaches actually do (descriptive propensities), not on what an xWPA engine believes is optimal. It also simplifies the core pipeline and hopefully improves tail calibration where coaches rarely kick.
Practical Advantages
	Fewer moving parts, same (or better) selection correction.
IPW needs π(X), not xWPA. We avoid fitting and maintaining multiple WP heads before training the main model.
	Aligns with observed behavior.
π(X) learns real-world thresholds (distance, yards-to-go, time/score, weather) not the “rational” thresholds implied by xWPA.
	Stronger tail performance.
IPW boosts under-observed states (e.g., long, late, windy). AIPW lets us evaluate across the whole space, including non-attempts.

Scope & data handling (brief)
	Seasons: 2015–2024 (Q1–Q4). PATs included (treated as certain attempts). Blocks counted as misses.
	Rolling-origin out-of-fold (OOF) for π̂ (attempt model) across seasons to avoid leakage and capture era drift.
Example: train 2012-2014 → predict 2015; …; train 2021-2023 → predict 2024.
	Base M1 (no IPW): cloglog GLMM with distance spline, weather, season, (1|kicker) and (1|stadium), potential (Kicker|Season) to capture seasonal drift, plus other factors (leverage)
	M2 (IPW): same success model, but each attempted kick weighted by w = 1/π̂ (stabilized, clipped). Control-function ON by default (add π̂ as a centered covariate).
Optional experiment (M3): Carefully include non-attempts with cross-fitted pseudo-labels (xFĜ) and small influence weights (λ), only if it improves AIPW calibration in low-overlap regions without hurting kicks-only metrics.

Key formulas (kept minimal, to support the narrative)
1) Attempt propensity (rolling-origin OOF)
We fit π ̂(X)=P(A=1∣X) on all kickable 4th-down snaps (A=1 if a FG was attempted; A=0 otherwise), with features:
	Distance & yardline splines, yards-to-go, score difference, log1p(time_remaining) + late-game spline, weather, season; maybe random intercept for coach-season.
OOF: for each season (t), train on past 3 seasons, predict  π ̂ for season (t).
PATs: set  π ̂= 1.
2) IPW success model (M2; kicks only)
Fit the cloglog GLMM for make% on attempted kicks with weights
w_i=1/π ̂_i  "(stabilized,clipped)"

Add a control-function regressor (centered) to capture residual selection:
〖"make" 〗_i∼f("distance,weather,time,season")+(1∣"kicker")+(1∣"stadium")+((π ̃_i)┬⏟)┬("centered " π ̂_i )

3) AIPW evaluation (full decision space)
For every kickable state (kicks and non-kicks), compute the pseudo-outcome
ψ_i "  "="  " m ̂_i "  "+"  "  A_i/π ̂_i (Y_i-m ̂_i)∈[0,1]

Use ψ to compute Brier, ECE/MCE, and calibration curves by distance, wind, and late-game.
Interpretation: kicks (A=1) pull predictions toward truth Ywith leverage 1/π ̂; non-kicks (A=0) leave ψ=m ̂.
4) (Optional) Semi-supervised M3 (guard-railed)
	Cross-fit M1 to get out-of-fold (xFG) ̂for non-attempts only.
	Train one pass on kicks + non-attempts using soft targets and asymmetric weights:
■(&"Targets:" Y_i " for kicks" ,Y ̃_i=(xFG) ̂_i " for non-attempts" @&"Weights:" w_i^(("att" ))=1/π ̂_i ,w_i^(("non" ))=λ⋅(1-π ̂_i)/π ̂_i ," " λ∈{0.1,0.2,0.3})

Keep M3 only if AIPW shows a clear tail-calibration gain without degrading kicks-only performance.

Normalize total weight = N attempts
→ improves variance
→ improves calibration
